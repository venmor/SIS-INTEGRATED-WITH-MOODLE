# Role Blueprint 11, Part 1 — Quality Assurance Officer Review and Evidence Workspace

> **Source mode:** composite approved Part 1. It organizes Design Section 11, the approved role catalogue, permission rules and Parts 2–5 into the entry workflow required before findings/corrective action. No new regulatory framework or authority is introduced.

## Purpose

The Quality Assurance Officer (QAO) plans governed reviews, maps criteria to evidence, coordinates responses and records an evidence-based assessment. Metrics and AI may identify questions or summarize cited sources, but cannot create a finding, close an action or submit a regulatory return.

## Workspace

Header identifies active role, organizational/programme scope and review cycle. Navigation contains review calendar, assigned reviews, evidence requests, criterion assessment, findings/actions, programme reviews, committee packages, reporting obligations and archive.

The home page prioritizes:

- Reviews needing plan approval
- Evidence requests awaiting response
- Criteria with incomplete or stale evidence
- Conflicts of interest or missing independent reviewer
- Draft assessments needing factual verification
- Deadlines and external obligations at risk

## Journey — initiate and assess a quality review

1. Select an approved review type, framework/version, scope, period, owner and due dates.
2. Confirm reviewer assignment, conflict declarations and permission to access necessary evidence.
3. Map each criterion to requested evidence and responsible source owner.
4. Source owners upload/link evidence, provide context and declare completeness; the QAO cannot silently edit their source records.
5. The system records evidence origin, classification, version, effective period and access history.
6. The QAO assesses each criterion as met, partly met, not met or not applicable only where the framework permits, with reasons and cited evidence.
7. Factual clarification is routed to the owner; assessment independence is retained.
8. Once review evidence is sufficient, a proposed finding may be created through Part 2.

## Evidence boundaries

The QAO sees the minimum information needed for the assigned review. Restricted counselling, safeguarding, payment credentials and unrelated personal data remain hidden or supplied through approved aggregated/redacted evidence. A QAO who authored a source cannot act as independent verifier where policy requires separation.

## Status model

Planned → Evidence collection → Assessment → Programme/owner response → Findings/actions → Independent verification → Committee/approval → Closed/archived. Returned, paused, superseded and cancelled states require reasons and do not delete history.

## Failure and recovery

- Missing/stale evidence is explicit and blocks unsupported conclusions.
- A source update creates a new evidence version and flags affected assessment.
- Access revoked during review blocks further viewing and records the change.
- Duplicate uploads/requests are linked rather than creating parallel obligations.
- Export/submission failure preserves the package and records delivery/retry separately.
- AI summary without citations or with restricted content is rejected.

## Acceptance requirements

- Framework, scope, period, owner and criterion versions are explicit.
- Evidence source, classification, freshness and access are traceable.
- Findings require human assessment and cited evidence.
- Source ownership and independent verification conflicts are enforced.
- Restricted data is minimized/redacted.
- Parts 2–5 can consume the review without redefining its evidence.
- Authorization, audit, stale-data, conflict, accessibility and recovery tests pass.
