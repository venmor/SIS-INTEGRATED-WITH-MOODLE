# Visual Quality and “Does Not Feel AI-Generated” Review

## Page purpose

- Can the user state the page’s one primary goal?
- Are workspace, scope and academic period visible where required?
- Does status explain state, reason, owner/next step and last update?
- Is the primary action specific rather than vague (`Submit application`, not `Continue`)?

## Visual restraint

- No gradients, glass effects, oversized welcome banners or random illustrations.
- No decorative metric cards, unexplained charts or repeated card grids.
- No chatbot/floating assistant unless separately approved.
- One component pattern represents one kind of information.
- Icons support labels; critical actions are not icon-only.
- Spacing groups decisions and evidence rather than making the page artificially empty.

## Real workflow states

- Loading, empty, stale, validation, permission denied, conflict and recovery are designed.
- Important forms save drafts and preserve valid input after failure where allowed.
- High-impact actions use a review page and describe consequences.
- Disabled actions explain why and how to become eligible.
- External uncertainty says “recorded/queued/awaiting confirmation,” not false success.

## Accessibility and responsiveness

- Keyboard order follows visual order; focus is visible and restored correctly.
- Labels remain visible; error summary links to fields.
- Status is not colour-only; contrast and zoom/reflow are checked.
- Tables transform to labelled mobile records without losing meaning.
- No critical interaction depends on hover, drag or animation.
- Screen-reader announcements cover saves, errors and asynchronous status changes.

## Content

- Sentence case and plain language.
- Message sequence: what happened → what it means → what to do next.
- Dates, time zones, amounts, references and deadlines are unambiguous.
- Internal notes and user-visible communication are clearly separated.
- Sensitive information is minimized/masked.

A screen fails review if it is visually attractive but cannot prove these behaviours.
